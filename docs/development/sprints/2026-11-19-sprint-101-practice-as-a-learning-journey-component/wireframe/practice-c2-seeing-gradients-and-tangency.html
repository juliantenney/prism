Seeing gradients and tangency

Use the fixed-perimeter rectangle to decide which movements improve area, which preserve the constraint, and why the gradients align at a regular constrained stationary point.

Assumes prior journey work on why the constraint matters. Record judgements before opening feedback. Advisory ~25–35 min

Problem in play

Maximise area \(A(x,y)=xy\) for positive side lengths subject to fixed perimeter \(2x+2y=20\), equivalently \(x+y=10\).

General gradients: \(\nabla A(x,y)=(y,x)\) and \(\nabla g(x,y)=(1,1)\) when \(g(x,y)=x+y\). A feasible tangent direction is \(\mathbf{t}=(1,-1)\).

This compact setup is intentional. A full knowledge summary is omitted because the journey already framed the optimisation setting; the formulae here are retained only as working apparatus for the activities.

Activity 1

Predict increase versus feasibility

Figure. Constant-area curves \(xy=16\), \(xy=24\) and the perimeter line \(x+y=10\). No feasibility labels are supplied.

Classify each marked point and decide whether a small step in the direction of \(\nabla A\) from \((4,6)\) preserves the perimeter.

Hint

Check \(x+y\) and \(xy\) independently. Then compute the change in \(x+y\) under \((\Delta x,\Delta y)=\varepsilon(6,4)\).

Feedback

\((2,8)\) and \((4,6)\) both satisfy \(x+y=10\) (areas \(16\) and \(24\)). \((6,6)\) has larger area \(36\) but \(x+y=12\), so it is not admissible.

At \((4,6)\), \(\nabla A=(6,4)\). A step \(\varepsilon(6,4)\) changes the coordinate sum by \(10\varepsilon\neq 0\), so steepest unrestricted increase is not feasible. A feasible direction such as \((1,-1)\) preserves \(x+y\).

Revision cue: if your explanation fused “area rises” with “allowed by the constraint”, rewrite so each claim has its own evidence.

Activity 2

Assign tangent and normal roles

Figure. Candidate directions at \((4,6)\) on \(xy=24\) and \(x+y=10\). Roles are for you to establish by calculation.

For each vector, decide its relationship to \(\nabla A=(6,4)\) or \(\nabla g=(1,1)\) using a dot-product check—not the look of the diagram.

Vector Your classification Dot-product check you would write
\((6,4)\)
\((4,-6)\)
\((1,1)\)
\((1,-1)\)

Hint

On a regular level set, \(\nabla f\cdot\mathbf{t}=0\) for a tangent \(\mathbf{t}\). Check \((6,4)\cdot(4,-6)\) and \((1,1)\cdot(1,-1)\).

Feedback

\((6,4)=\nabla A\) is normal to the objective level curve. \((4,-6)\) is tangent to \(xy=24\) because \((6,4)\cdot(4,-6)=0\). \((1,1)=\nabla g\) is normal to the constraint. \((1,-1)\) is a feasible tangent because \((1,1)\cdot(1,-1)=0\).

Moving along the objective tangent keeps area stationary to first order. Moving along the constraint tangent keeps the perimeter stationary to first order. Those are different conserved quantities—so the two tangents are not interchangeable.

Activity 3

First-order change and gradient alignment

At feasible points \((4,6)\) and \((5,5)\), investigate \(\nabla A\cdot\mathbf{t}\) with \(\mathbf{t}=(1,-1)\). Then decide what the vanishing of that directional derivative does—and does not—establish.

Use \(\nabla A(x,y)=(y,x)\) and \(\mathbf{t}=(1,-1)\).

Hint

\(\nabla A(4,6)=(6,4)\) and \(\nabla A(5,5)=(5,5)\). If every feasible tangent gives zero first-order objective change, \(\nabla A\) is normal to the feasible tangent—and so is \(\nabla g\) when \(\nabla g\neq\mathbf{0}\).

Feedback

\(\nabla A(4,6)\cdot(1,-1)=6-4=2\neq 0\): a first-order feasible change in area is available. \(\nabla A(5,5)\cdot(1,-1)=5-5=0\): no first-order feasible improvement along \(\mathbf{t}\).

At a regular equality-constrained local stationary point, \(\nabla A\cdot\mathbf{t}=0\) for feasible tangents \(\mathbf{t}\), so \(\nabla A\) and \(\nabla g\) are parallel: \(\nabla A=\lambda\nabla g\). That is a necessary first-order condition under regularity—not by itself a proof of a global maximum.

Revision cue: if you treated vanishing first-order change as a global optimality certificate, revise the scope sentence.

Activity 4

Transfer across equivalent constraints

At \((5,5)\), the same rectangles can be written \(g_1=x+y=10\) or \(g_2=2x+2y=20\). Decide what changes and what must stay geometrically the same.

Hint

\(g_2=2g_1\) scales the constraint gradient, so \(\lambda\) rescales, but the feasible tangent and the requirement \(\nabla A\cdot\mathbf{t}=0\) are unchanged.

Feedback

\(\nabla g_2=(2,2)\). With \(\nabla A(5,5)=(5,5)\), \(\lambda=2.5\) satisfies \(\nabla A=\lambda\nabla g_2\). Relative to \(g_1\), the multiplier would be \(5\). The feasible set and feasible tangents are unchanged; only the scaling of the normal representation changes.

The alignment condition is necessary for a regular constrained local stationary point. It does not, by itself, prove a global maximum of area on the perimeter constraint.
