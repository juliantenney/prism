## Cross-case findings (EVIDENCE — not final architecture)

1. Assessment Pack is commissioned where the principal job becomes **deliberate elicitation of interpretable evidence of an integrated learner capability**.
2. Elicitation / evidence / feedback / judgement / reflection / revision / evaluation / independence / authentic performance do **not** individually imply Assessment Pack.
3. Interactive remains appropriate when consequential reasoning/action/revision is principally how capability is **developed**.
4. Situated Task remains appropriate when purposeful activity in authentic context is the principal learning vehicle, even if evidence is later reused.
5. Assessment Pack demand includes **sustained constructed performance** — not reducible to conventional item forms.
6. A **determinate correct answer is not required**.
7. Assessment Pack may **consume/reconnect** with earlier learner work without owning/recreating the producing activity.
8. **Integrated performance** can be educationally essential; atomising it may destroy the capability elicited.
9. Feedback remains **formative**; NON-SUMMATIVE invariant held across all three journeys.
10. Live LJ conceptual demand ≫ current closed-form implementation — gap, not proof capability exists.

## Recurring patterns observed (NOT formalised)

- Stopping points late or mid when **integrated capability evidence** is the job.
- Constructed / judgement performance without single keys.
- Sibling products absorb “assessment-like” activity when the centre of gravity is development or situated doing.
- Handoffs from Situated/Interactive into Assessment without state-transport product.

Surprising / confirmatory: Case C kept judgement, investigation, and metacognition in Interactive until a single culminating Assessment Pack — strong negative evidence against “any judgement → Assessment Pack”.

## Proposed briefs (approved text)

### Brief A

```text
Design a substantial Learning Journey for second-year undergraduate students on a social or health sciences programme who need to become competent at reasoning about cause and effect when working with observational (non-experimental) evidence.

Learners already have introductory statistics and can read a simple results table, but they typically confuse association with causation, over-trust single studies, and struggle to say what would strengthen or weaken a causal claim about real-world interventions (for example in public health, education, or social policy).

Available learning time is approximately 18–25 hours across 3–4 weeks of a module, combining independent study with two short taught seminars. Learners work individually for most of the journey. They will later use this learning when evaluating claims in coursework and when reading empirical papers in subsequent modules.

The journey should develop genuine understanding over time — not a one-sitting overview of terminology. Use whatever mix of learning experiences is educationally necessary for this progression. Do not assume a particular product mix.
```

### Brief B

```text
Design a substantial professional development Learning Journey for experienced staff in a service organisation (for example healthcare, higher education professional services, local government, or customer-facing operations) who need to improve the quality and reliability of handovers between colleagues.

Learners already perform handovers as part of their job. The problem is not lack of a policy document; it is that handovers in practice are incomplete, inconsistent, or fail under time pressure, and teams often lack a shared way of noticing what is going wrong and improving it in their own setting.

Available learning time is approximately 12–16 hours over 3–4 weeks, mostly in the learner’s own workplace alongside normal duties, with limited protected study time. Learners should use their real work context. Access to some workplace information may be imperfect; privacy and professional boundaries must be respected.

The journey should help learners investigate what actually happens in their setting, try improvements that they own, and make sense of what they learn so that better practice can continue after the journey ends. Use whatever mix of learning experiences is educationally necessary. Do not assume a particular product mix or sequence.
```

### Brief C

```text
Design a substantial Learning Journey for adult learners (final-year undergraduates or early-career professionals) who need to make careful judgements about contested claims they encounter online — for example claims about health, technology, public policy, or workplace practice circulating in articles, posts, or shared documents.

Learners can already find information quickly and often feel confident in their instincts, but they struggle when sources conflict, when evidence is selective, or when a claim is framed persuasively. The aim is not to teach a single checklist to apply mechanically; it is to develop more disciplined interpretation and justification so that learners can explain what they trust, what they doubt, and why — including when a responsible answer is that the claim cannot yet be settled.

Available learning time is approximately 10–14 hours over 2–3 weeks of independent study. Learners work mainly alone. They should finish able to approach new contested claims with better judgement, not merely to recall examples used in the journey.

Use whatever mix of learning experiences is educationally necessary for developing that judgement. Do not assume a particular product mix.
```
