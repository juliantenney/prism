## Instructional vs editorial architecture (final WP1 statement)

| Layer | Judges |
| ----- | ------ |
| **Instructional architecture** | Whether the resource constructs the **commissioned** understanding, in suitable order, with sufficient explanation and representation — **including purpose-fit and epistemic-form preservation** |
| **Editorial architecture** | Whether the reading experience is authored: entry, pacing, emphasis, transitions, representation participation, controlled recurrence, hierarchy, typography, closure |

**Test question:** Can the quality model explain mediocrity even when a resource is factually correct, pedagogically sensible and technically valid?

**Answer after five cases:**

- **Yes**, primarily via editorial-architecture failures, when purpose-fit holds (**C01–C03, C05**): instructional typically STRONG; editorial CONCERN (furniture, dual close, flat hierarchy, residual cadence, soft integration).
- **Also yes differently** when purpose-fit fails (**C04**): instructional CONCERN through neighbouring-brief / epistemic-form loss despite internal coherence for an alternate lesson; editorial CONCERN remains.

Do **not** reduce editorial architecture to visual polish.

## Mediocrity-despite-correctness (explained)

A resource can be sound, well sequenced for *some* lesson, sufficiently explanatory for that lesson, and functionally rendered, yet still fall short because:

1. it was authored toward a **neighbouring** intellectual object (**EQ1** / C04); or  
2. the **composition of the reading experience** fails — prospectus furniture, equal rhetorical weight, accompanying rather than participating artefacts, repeated closures, flat hierarchy (**EQ2–EQ8** / C01–C05).
